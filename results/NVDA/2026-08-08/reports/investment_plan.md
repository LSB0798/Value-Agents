

I weighed this as a trade, not just a thesis. The bull side has the cleaner hard evidence: NVDA reported Q3 FY2026 revenue of $96.2B, revenue growth around 106% year over year, roughly 75% gross margins, 63.7% net margins, 117% ROE, a very strong balance sheet, and no reported major design loss or order cut. The demand story is broadening through Microsoft, Alphabet, sovereign projects, and full-stack AI factory packaging, including the reported $3B Lancium power investment. The bear side has the strongest risk warnings: NVDA is expensive, the demand base is still concentrated, neocloud stress in CoreWeave and Nebius could become a credit and order-deferral issue, custom silicon from Broadcom and AMD threatens inference and large-customer share, receivables are about $63.1B, free cash flow normalized from a $48.6B peak to $21.4B, insiders are net sellers, and the technical setup near the $236 high leaves little room for error.

My recommendation is Buy NVDA, but not as a reckless chase. I am aligning with the bull because the confirmed fundamentals and absence of a demand break are more actionable than the bear’s forward-looking credit and substitution concerns. The strongest argument is that NVDA is not just a GPU supplier; it is becoming the operating layer for AI factories, and that creates switching costs, attach-rate expansion, and pricing power. The bear’s concerns should be treated as monitoring triggers, not reasons to stay out unless the setup breaks.

The rationale is that the highest-conviction evidence in the debate is confirmed revenue, margin quality, hyperscaler capex, and technical structure. The bear case depends on a neocloud credit event, an ASIC design win, an Alphabet capex miss, or a technical breakdown, none of which has happened yet. Valuation is real, but PEG near 0.28 and 106% revenue growth mean the stock is not obviously overpaid if the build-out continues. The main mistake to avoid is treating NVDA as a forever hold because it is a strong trend. The trade must be managed with entries, stops, and catalyst-based exits. Since there are no past reflections provided, I am applying the usual lessons from similar situations: do not anchor to the all-time high, do not ignore customer-credit risk, do not let a good thesis become an oversized position, and do not wait for perfection when the trend and fundamentals are still intact.

The watchlist should include hyperscaler AI data-center spending, Alphabet’s infrastructure decision, CoreWeave and Nebius earnings, Broadcom and AMD custom-silicon progress, power-related bottlenecks, and NVDA insider selling. In short, I am buying NVDA because the fundamental trend is still stronger than the bear’s damage scenario, but I am doing it with staged entries, strict stops, and catalyst triggers so the position remains a controlled trade rather than a passive bet on AI euphoria.
