## 1. Company Profile & Valuation Snapshot

NVIDIA is the dominant designer of AI/data-center GPUs, and its scale has grown to a **$5.52 trillion market cap** — the largest in the market. Key headline metrics:

- **TTM Revenue:** ~$303B
- **TTM Net Income:** ~$192.9B
- **TTM Net Margin:** 63.7% — an exceptional, software-like margin for a hardware company
- **ROE:** 117.2% — elite return on capital
- **PEG Ratio:** 0.28 — valuation is extremely cheap relative to earnings growth
- **P/B:** 24.1x (consistent with $229B equity vs. $5.52T market cap)
- **TTM Free Cash Flow:** ~$41.8B
- **52-week Range:** $164.27–$243.37; **50-DMA:** $222.59; **200-DMA:** $202.34 — the stock trades **above both moving averages**, confirming an established uptrend

## 2. Income Statement Trends (Q1 2025 → Q3 2026)

| Metric | Q1 2025 | Q3 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $46.7B | $96.2B | +106% (12 months) |
| Gross Profit | $33.9B | $72.1B | +113% |
| Operating Income | $28.4B | $63.7B | +124% |
| R&D Expense | $4.3B | $7.1B | +65% |
| Net Income | $26.4B | $59.7B | +126% |
| Diluted EPS | $1.08 | $2.46 | +128% |

**Key observations:**
- **Revenue more than doubled** in 12 months ($46.7B → $96.2B), reflecting sustained AI accelerator demand.
- **Gross margin ~75%** ($72.1B / $96.2B) — pricing power remains intact even at enormous scale.
- **Operating leverage is strong:** operating income grew faster than revenue, and R&D rose only ~65% vs. +106% revenue growth.
- **Important nuance:** Q3 2026 net income includes **$7.8B of gains on security sales** (non-operating). Ex-normalized, EBITDA is **$65.1B** — traders should model core earnings separately from mark-to-market gains when building forward EPS estimates.

**Insight for traders:** The growth rate itself (not just the level) is the story — 100%+ YoY revenue growth at a $300B revenue base is historically unprecedented. Watch the *quarter-over-quarter* deceleration/acceleration cadence as the primary signal; even modest QoQ growth will be read positively given the base effect.

| Current Assets / Liabilities | — | $197.4B / $43.0B | Working Capital $154.4B |
| Total Debt | — | $38.4B | LT debt $8.5B → $32.4B; current $1.0B |
| Financial Asset Investments | $3.8B | $51.2B | +$47.4B |

**Key observations:**
- **Fortress balance sheet.** Working capital of **$154.4B** and a current ratio of ~4.6x means zero near-term liquidity risk.
- **Net cash position is very large:** ~$62.5B cash/ST investments + $51.2B financial investments ≈ $113.7B vs. only $38.4B total debt → **~$75B net cash**. The 3.8x rise in long-term debt is immaterial at this equity scale.
- **Receivables ($63.1B) are ~21% of the quarter's revenue** — within one month of sales, consistent with hyperscaler customers and not a red flag. Inventory ($31.6B) is ~10% of revenue, healthy for this supply chain.
- **Equity nearly doubled YoY** to $229B, driven primarily by earnings retention.
- The jump in **financial asset investments ($3.8B → $51.2B)** explains part of the $7.8B gains on security sales — the company is actively managing its cash pool, which adds a recurring (but volatile) earnings component.

**Insight for traders:** The balance sheet removes financial risk from the investment case entirely. The only meaningful fundamental risk is *demand* risk, not solvency or liquidity.

**Key observations:**
- **Massive capital return program:** buybacks averaging ~$10B+/quarter plus a newly significant dividend ($6.0B in one quarter) — at a $5.5T market cap this represents a real, growing offset to dilution and a signal of management confidence.
- **FCF volatility is worth noting:** the drop from $48.6B (Q1 2026) to $21.4B (Q3 2026) reflects timing of investments and working-capital build, not operating weakness — OCF itself rose from $15.4B to $24.1B. Don't over-read the FCF normalization as an inflection.
- **CapEx is tiny** ($1.3–2.7B/quarter) relative to revenue — NVDA outsources manufacturing (foundry model), which is the structural source of its 63.7% net margin.

**Insight for traders:** Model OCF (~$24B+/quarter run rate) as the reliable cash-generation metric, not single-quarter FCF. Buyback pace is a useful tell for management's own view on valuation.

## 5. Bull Case, Risks & Watch Items

**Bull case (supported by evidence):**
1. PEG of 0.28 — even with a high absolute multiple, growth-adjusted valuation is cheap.
2. 63.7% net margin + 117% ROE — best-in-class profitability.
3. ~$75B net cash; no financial risk.
4. Revenue still growing >100% YoY with intact ~75% gross margins.
5. Technicals aligned: price above 50- and 200-day averages, near the top of its 52-week range ($164.27–$243.37).

**Risks & watch items:**
1. **$7.8B one-time gains on security sales** flatter Q3 2026 EPS ($2.46). Street estimates built off un-normalized results may be too high.
2. **Customer concentration** in a few hyperscalers — receivables build and any capex pause by customers would hit revenue faster than any competitor.
3. **FCF normalization** — if working-capital intensity keeps rising, cash conversion could compress.
4. **Momentum risk:** trading near the top of a 52-week range with a 24.1x book multiple means a guidance stumble produces outsized downside.

**Actionable triggers for traders:**
- Next print: focus on **normalized (ex-gains) operating income**, QoQ revenue growth rate, and any gross-margin commentary — not headline net income.
- Watch buyback authorization size as a valuation signal from management.
- Defensive posture if price loses the 50-DMA ($222.59); the 200-DMA ($202.34) is the trend line that matters.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

NVIDIA's fundamentals are in a once-in-a-decade state: revenue and earnings doubling in 12 months, ~75% gross margins, a net-cash balance sheet with $154B working capital, aggressive buybacks and a new dividend, and a PEG ratio (0.28) that suggests the market still prices the stock as "too expensive" relative to growth. The key caveat is the $7.8B of non-operating gains embedded in the latest quarter — normalize earnings before projecting forward. On the evidence, fundamentals support a **BUY**, with the 200-day moving average ($202.34) as the key trend-risk level.
