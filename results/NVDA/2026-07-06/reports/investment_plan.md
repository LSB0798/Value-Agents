

The bull case is stronger on confirmed evidence: NVDA is generating exceptional cash flow, holding extreme margins, growing revenue and earnings at a very high rate, and still trading at a forward multiple that looks low relative to that growth. The bear case is strongest on risk: the valuation has almost no cushion for a capex pause, competitor wins from AMD, Broadcom, and custom silicon are real, and the technical structure has not fully reclaimed the 50-day or VWMA. My decision is to align with the bull analyst and recommend Buy NVDA, but only as a staged, risk-managed trade. I am not treating this as a zero-risk buy; I am saying the confirmed financial strength and multi-year infrastructure demand make standing aside or selling too costly if the AI buildout continues.

The rationale is that NVDA’s cash generation is the key differentiator. The debate keeps circling valuation, but a business with the margin profile, free cash flow, and net-cash position described in the debate is not the same as a fragile cyclical chip stock simply because it has a high market cap. The bear is right that NVDA needs continued demand and margin durability, but the bull is right that the market is not pricing NVDA like a speculative bubble. A 13.9x forward earnings multiple and 0.28 PEG do not leave much room for a margin collapse, but they also do not prove the cycle is over. The more convincing path is to buy the strength of the cash flow story while respecting the bear’s technical warning that the correction has not fully resolved.

For the trader, I would not chase NVDA above the $201 VWMA without confirmation. I would start with one third of the planned position in the $190 to $195 area, using a hard stop on a daily close below $188. That stop keeps the initial trade risk around 3.5% and makes it clear that if the correction resumes, the thesis is wrong. I would add a second third only if NVDA holds the $190 zone and forms a higher low, and a final third only after a daily close above $201, with stronger conviction if it pushes toward $209. If NVDA closes below $188, I would exit and reassess; that would be my sell trigger. If it reclaims $201 and $209, I would trail the stop up to breakeven and then protect gains, with targets at $209, $220, and $235. For account sizing, I would keep the risk to the $188 stop at no more than about 1% of total portfolio equity, because NVDA can gap on capex, competition, or memory-cycle news.

If you already own NVDA, I would not add aggressively into $201 to $209 without a confirmed close. That resistance area is where the bear case says the rebound is unproven, so it is a better place to trim into strength or buy protection than to pile in. From similar setups, my mistake has been treating support as confirmation and adding too early before momentum actually turned. Here I am forcing myself to use a hard stop, staged entries, and confirmation before increasing risk. The decision is Buy NVDA, but only with disciplined execution.
