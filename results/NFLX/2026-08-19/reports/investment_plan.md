

I’m going with Buy NFLX, but only as a staged, risk-defined trade rather than a full position right at the current level. The strongest bull argument is that NFLX is a high-quality cash-flow franchise that has already been punished for execution worries, not a broken business. It still has a large revenue base, high operating and net margins, roughly 25 billion in trailing free cash flow, very modest net debt, and a forward multiple around 18.5x after a major drawdown. That makes the stock look much less like a speculative media story and more like a de-rated growth asset with real optionality from ads and live programming. The technicals are not perfect, but the reclaim of the 50-day, positive MACD, and recovery in RSI show that the immediate slide has paused and that there is at least a short-term bid.

The strongest bear argument is also real: NFLX is still below a falling 200-day, the 50-day is still declining, and the ad and live business has not yet proven it can scale without adding costs and pressuring margins. The Q3 guidance overhang matters, the DCF warning is not trivial, and the negative tangible book point shows that the equity value is dependent on franchise execution rather than a hard asset floor. If Q3 guidance disappoints or the stock loses the 72 to 74 support area, this rebound can turn back into a bear-market rally. So I am not ignoring the bear’s warning. I am just weighing it against the fact that the downside is now more about multiple compression and execution than survival, and the stock has already de-rated enough to create a more attractive risk-reward than at the highs.

My recommendation is Buy NFLX because the bull case has the more compelling combination of durable cash generation, a compressed multiple for this quality of business, and asymmetric optionality if ad and live revenue even partially scale. The bear case is strongest on timing and trend, but it is asking you to wait for near-perfect confirmation, which often means buying later at a higher price if the re-rating is real. My past lesson on similar situations is that I have been too willing to buy quality before price confirms, and also too quick to abandon quality after one ugly tape print. The fix here is to buy in stages, respect support, and use a hard invalidation instead of relying on narrative.

For the trader, the first action is to size the position so that a full stop-out below 68.95 risks no more than about one percent of total portfolio equity. If the average entry is near 75, a stop below 68.95 is roughly a nine to ten percent move on the position, which means the position should probably be in the eight to ten percent range of the portfolio, depending on account size and existing tech exposure. I would deploy the first third at 76 to 78 only if the daily close holds above 74.50, deploy the second third on a pullback to 73 to 75, and deploy the final third only if the stock reclaims and holds above 80 with a non-disappointing Q3 guide. If the stock gaps down through 72 before a Q3 catalyst, I would pause new buying and reassess rather than averaging down blindly.

The invalidation is a daily close below 68.95. That level is the line that tells us the bounce failed and the path back toward 65 and then 60 is open, so the trade should be exited at that point, not defended. If the stock reaches 80, I would move the stop to breakeven or 75 and take partial profit into 82. If it reaches 85, I would trail the stop to 78 or 80 and let the rest run toward the 200-day area at 88 to 89. If NFLX reaches the 88 to 89 zone while the 50-day is still falling, I would sell at least half the position because the bear’s main technical objection would still be valid. If the 50-day flattens and price closes above 89 with volume, the remainder can be held for a longer re-rating through Q3.

The key monitoring triggers are Q3 guidance, ad revenue and live programming commentary, content cost trend, subscriber additions, and whether the 50-day stops declining. If Q3 guide is flat or weak on EPS, I would cut the position quickly even if price is still above 72, because the thesis depends on execution not breaking. If the guide is decent and the stock holds above 74, I would add as planned. If NFLX is sold on macro or AI rotation but keeps the 72 to 74 shelf and fundamentals remain intact, that is an add, not an exit. In plain terms, I am buying NFLX because the cash machine is de-rated and the optionality is cheap relative to the quality, but I am respecting the bear’s warning by making the trade conditional on support and Q3 proof.
