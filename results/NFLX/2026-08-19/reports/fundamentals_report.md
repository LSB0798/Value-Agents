## 2. Income Statement Analysis (Quarterly, Q2 2025 → Q2 2026)

**Top-line growth is the core bull case:**
- Revenue grew from **$11.08B (Q2 2025) to $12.56B (Q2 2026) — +13.4% YoY**, demonstrating sustained double-digit growth even at this scale.
- Operating income has ranged **$2.96B–$4.19B** per quarter, supporting the 33%+ operating margin.
- **Q1 2026 was an outlier quarter:** net income peaked at **$5.28B**, boosted by a **$2.85B interest income spike**, inflating EBITDA to a high of **$11.13B**. This one-off item means Q1 earnings *overstate* normalized run-rate.
- Net income **normalized to $3.40B in Q2 2026** — the more representative quarterly earnings level (~$13.6B TTM net income).
- Diluted EPS has ranged **$0.56–$1.23** per quarter; traders should anchor to the ~$0.80–$1.00 normalized range rather than the Q1 2026 spike.
- Cost of revenue ($5.33B–$6.52B/quarter, driven largely by content spend) remains the dominant cost, but scales well with revenue — content-as-cost-of-revenue leverage is working in Netflix's favor.
- Operating expenses of $2.09B–$2.57B/quarter are modest relative to revenue, indicating good operating discipline.

**Trading insight:** Model earnings on ~$3.4B/quarter normalized net income (not the Q1 2026 $5.28B). At a forward P/E of 18.53x, the market is pricing continued growth; any upside surprise in guidance or a re-acceleration in FCF could trigger multiple re-rating.

## 4. Cash Flow Analysis (Q1 2025 → Q2 2026)

This is the strongest part of the story:

- **Operating cash flow:** $1.74B–$5.29B per quarter, peaking at **$5.29B in Q1 2026**.
- **Free cash flow:** $1.53B–$5.09B per quarter despite only modest CapEx (**$156M–$239M/quarter**) — an asset-light model with exceptional cash conversion.
- **Shareholder returns are aggressive:** buybacks of **$1.27B–$4.71B per quarter** — at the midpoint (~$3B/quarter), annualized buybacks of ~$12B = **~4% of market cap per year** in equity reduction, a powerful per-share accretion engine.
- **Capital structure discipline:** **No debt issuance or repayment in the last four quarters** (following a $1.03B repayment in Q2 2025). Netflix is now *net deleveraging or flat* while funding buybacks and acquisitions entirely from operations.
- **Cash position:** fluctuated $8.18B–$12.27B, ending Q2 2026 at **$9.10B** — comfortable liquidity even after heavy repurchases.
- Investing outflows include the **$586M business acquisition in Q1 2026**, signaling a re-engagement with M&A — watch for deal announcements as potential catalysts.

## 5. Synthesis & Actionable Insights

**Bull case (dominant):**
1. **13.4% YoY revenue growth at $48B+ scale** — rare for a company this size; streaming/ad/interactive content expansion still has runway.
2. **FCF yield of ~8.7%** — among the best in mega-cap tech; provides a valuation floor and funds ~4%/year buyback accretion.
3. **Forward P/E 18.53x < Trailing 22.16x** and PEG 1.21 — growth is priced fairly, not frothy; the de-rating below the 200-day MA may be an entry opportunity rather than a warning.
4. **Self-funding capital allocation:** buybacks + M&A + flat debt, no external financing needed.
5. **49.5% ROE and 33.4% operating margins** — world-class profitability compounding over time.

**Risks / watch items:**
1. **Earnings noise:** Q1 2026's $2.85B interest income spike distorted results; Q2's $3.40B is the anchor. Earnings-beats driven by non-ops items can mislead short-term traders.
2. **Technical weakness:** stock below 200-day MA suggests recent downside momentum; consider scaling in rather than lump-sum entry if momentum is a concern.
3. **Negative tangible book** — equity value depends entirely on franchise economics (subscriber growth, pricing power, content ROI).
4. **Content cost inflation** (cost of revenue $5.33B–$6.52B/quarter) is the key variable to monitor — any re-acceleration in content spend without matching revenue growth would compress the 33% operating margin.
5. **M&A appetite** ($586M deal in Q1 2026) is new — integration execution is a minor but real risk.

**Bottom line:** NFLX combines double-digit growth, elite cash generation (~$25.4B TTM FCF), aggressive buybacks, and a manageable balance sheet at a reasonable multiple (18.5x forward). The fundamentals strongly support **BUY**, with the primary risk being near-term technical momentum and content-cost inflation. Position sizing should account for the below-200-day-MA trend; a staggered entry or dip-buying approach is reasonable.
