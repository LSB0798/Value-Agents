## 4. Actionable Insights for Traders

1. **HOLD core positions; do not chase the bounce until tech stabilizes.** Last week's rotation into Dow components + AI-bubble chatter means AMZN's multiple is being re-underwritten. Wait for two confirmations before adding: (a) a week in which mega-cap tech holds above its 20-day, and (b) Q2-style capex commentary that ties spending to visible revenue (AWS utilization/pricing), not just GPU count.
2. **Position around June 23 Prime Day.** An earlier-than-usual Prime Day is a deliberate demand-capture move against renewed inflation. Trade the narrative *before* the event (positioning week of 6/15–6/23), then judge execution via 3–5 day web traffic/Prime signup headlines post-event. If Prime Day coverage is muted or "discount-dependent," treat it as a demand-warning and trim.
3. **The $2.5B settlement is a bounded, one-time P&L event (~low single-digit bps of revenue)** — not a thesis-breaker, but it will cap headline sentiment into any Q2 print. Don't overreact to the settlement itself; do react to what the settlement says about Prime workforce compliance going forward.
4. **Watch the Seattle data-center moratorium as an early-warning series.** One city is noise; if state/federal siting, permitting or power-grid regulation spreads, AMZN's capex ramp — the *engine* of its AI story — slows, and AWS's scarcity premium erodes. Monitor this as a fundamental (not headline) risk.
5. **Berkshire's exit is a sentiment, not a liquidity, event** for a stock of AMZN's size, but it removes a "floor buyer" narrative in the post-Buffett era. Pair it with the Meta equity-raise signal: the market is starting to question *who funds the capex*. AMZN's answer must be free cash flow, not the raise — watch operating CF vs. capex spread in the next earnings.
6. **Hedge structure:** with tech beta high (S&P tech +40% in 10 weeks), a long AMZN / short broader tech (or long AMZN / short a high-capex, weak-cash-flow peer) isolates the AWS-acceleration thesis from the sector drawdown risk.
