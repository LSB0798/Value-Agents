But I think your case is still too cautious about the upside and too focused on avoiding loss at the expense of opportunity. You talk about exiting the tactical layer or keeping only a separately governed strategic core. For a tactical book, that starts to sound like a full SELL with better language. If we do that, we hand back the upside in a stock that the technical report still sees as a pullback in an uptrend, with entries on a dip into 690 to 700 or a breakout above 728, targets to 779 and then 820 to 840, and a reasonably defined risk around the 655 to 639 area. That is not a broken setup. It is a setup where the risk reward is not attractive enough at 720 to justify full exposure, but still good enough to justify a smaller, managed position.

You also give too much weight to the capex warning without enough balance from the same fundamental report. Yes, capex jumped to about 30.1 billion in the quarter, free cash flow compressed, construction in progress is around 80.3 billion, total debt is around 112.3 billion, net debt is around 68.2 billion, and R&D more than doubled. That is real. But operating cash flow is still very strong, in the mid-to-high 20s to low 30s billions range, net margin is near 30 percent, revenue just printed at an all-time-high 60.8 billion, the current ratio is 2.226, and the forward multiple is about 20.8 with a PEG near 0.98. That does not mean META is risk free, but it does mean the market is not paying a bubble multiple for perfection. The downside has more cushion than a pure de-rating story implies. If we exit everything into a pause in an intact uptrend, we may be overreacting to a sustainability warning that the balance sheet can still absorb.

The macro backdrop is where I think the conservative case is strongest. Rising yields are a real headwind for a long-duration growth name, even a megacap. Soft U.S. and China consumer spending is a real risk for an advertising-dependent business. Geopolitical risk adds to the chance of broader beta moves. The AI monetization debate is becoming mainstream, and that cuts directly at the premium attached to META’s AI commerce story. In that environment, I do not want to keep full tactical exposure either. But the answer is not to treat the macro warning as proof that the stock must break down. The answer is to reduce risk enough that the macro can do its work without forcing a bad decision.

The balanced strategy is to take the trader’s SELL direction and make it sustainable. If we already own short-term or momentum-driven META exposure, I would trim the speculative layer into the 720 to 745 area, with the cleanest reduction coming near 742 to 745 if the market gives us that bounce. If we do not get the bounce, we scale down rather than wait for perfection. The objective is not to sell every share into the highest possible print. The objective is to cut the part of the position that is no longer doing clean tactical work.

I would keep only a small residual, maybe 20 to 30 percent of the original tactical size, and only if that size is small enough that a move to 685, 655, or 639 does not force a panic decision. If the position is too large to hold through that kind of move, we cut it more. If the firm has a separate strategic view on Meta, that core belongs in the strategic book, not the tactical book. The tactical book should not be carrying an emotional position just because we do not want to miss a continuation.

If we do not own META, I would not initiate a new full long at 720. The entry is not clean enough, the narrative is not clean enough, and the macro is not clean enough. The better paths are conditional. A volume-backed reclaim of 742 to 745 with a MACD flip would be one path, because that would invalidate the short-term bearish case. A stabilized dip into 690 to 700 where RSI still holds 50 and ad revenue or cash flow is not deteriorating would be another path. A deeper de-rate into 630 to 650 would be the most attractive area for the bull case, but only after stabilization and better evidence that capex is being controlled, free cash flow is stabilizing, ad revenue is holding, and Muse or agentic commerce is showing real traction. I would not chase a gap. I would not try to be first. I would wait for the chart and the data to reduce the uncertainty.

The levels should do the work. A close below 685 should force another reduction, not another debate. A close below 655 should be treated as the tactical trade breaking down. A loss of the 50 day around 639 should remove most or all of the remaining short-term exposure. On the upside, a reclaim of 742 to 745 on real volume should make us more bullish because it invalidates the short-term bearish case. A stabilization in 690 to 700 with RSI holding 50 and no deterioration in ad revenue or cash flow should allow a small add. The 630 to 650 zone is the best place to become meaningfully more bullish, but only after stabilization and better evidence.

If we keep a small core, options can be a secondary tool, not the main solution. A modest protective put spread below 685 can make sense, but it should be sized to the residual position and should not become the excuse for keeping a position that is still too large. A covered call above 745 can be useful only if we are genuinely willing to cap upside at resistance, but in a name that can gap on Muse data or earnings, it is not a neutral comfort structure. A long volatility position into earnings or Muse data can make sense because the sentiment report says the tape is in a fully priced, narrative-sensitive mode, but it should be modest and secondary to reducing the underlying directional risk.

The other part of the balanced view is diversification. The cash we create by trimming META should not automatically become more concentrated AI risk. We can redeploy that capital into lower-beta megacap tech, other parts of the AI infrastructure chain, or simply cash equivalents while we wait for a cleaner setup. The AI capex theme is still live, but META is one of the most exposed combinations of high beta, high capex, and narrative sensitivity right now. A moderate strategy should not just manage the META position; it should manage the portfolio’s overall exposure to the same macro and narrative risks.

So my final adjustment is not a full SELL and not a passive HOLD. It is a managed partial SELL into strength, with a small capped residual, hard reduction triggers, and re-entry only on better evidence or better price. That gives us the aggressive analyst’s discipline around not paying full price for an unproven AI commerce payoff. It gives us the conservative analyst’s respect for the fact that META is not a broken company and that a short thesis can be refuted quickly. And it keeps the firm from either getting trapped in a stretched long or getting left out of a clean continuation. That is the balanced, risk-adjusted path.
