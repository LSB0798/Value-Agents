That does not mean Apple is wrong long term. It means the stock may not get the easy liquidity tailwind it had during the last phase of the rally. If the market is rotating into AI infrastructure and AI winners, holding Apple near its high after a demand scare is a relative value mistake unless the position is properly reduced.

The buyback argument is important, but it supports the aggressive plan more than the hold plan. Apple is buying back stock, yes. The fundamental report shows a massive capital return program, with quarterly buybacks in the range of 12.3 to 25.1 billion dollars and dividends around 3.8 to 4.0 billion dollars per quarter. That is structural support. But management can afford to let the stock find a lower entry. If Apple washes out toward 322, that is a better entry. If it gets to 300 to 310, that is a much better entry. If it reaches the 296 to 290 zone, that is where the long-term risk reward becomes genuinely attractive. The conservative and neutral views keep using buybacks as a reason not to exit, but I would turn that around. Buybacks are a reason not to panic sell into a deep flush. They are not a reason to hold full size into a product launch and earnings print while the stock is near the high end of its range. The buyback floor is a floor for future accumulation, not a ceiling for current risk.

The technicals also support the aggressive view more than either of the other plans. The stock has rallied 33 percent over six months, made a cycle high at 345.34, and is now consolidating near the high end of the range with momentum cooling. MACD is still positive, yes, but the histogram is negative and the rate of momentum has reset. RSI is around 56, not oversold, which means the stock still has room to drift lower without triggering a panic bid. ATR has compressed from about 8.91 to 6.75, which means volatility is coiling. In a setup like that, with a fresh negative catalyst, you do not want to be full size hoping for a perfect top. You want to be selling strength. The medium-term trend is intact, but that does not make the short-term risk reward attractive. You can believe the long-term trend is bullish and still be wrong about holding full exposure near the highs through a negative catalyst window.

The high reward path here is not to hold half of a stretched position and hope the foldable launch and earnings pass cleanly. The high reward path is to sell the risk while there is still liquidity, preserve capital, and reenter on confirmation if the bull case proves itself. If the stock breaks down, we avoid the drawdown and can redeploy into either a lower AAPL entry or a stronger setup elsewhere. If the stock rallies despite the news, we have already reduced risk and can reenter above 345.34 with confirmation. The cost of being wrong is an upside move, yes. But the invalidation is clear: a decisive reclaim above 345.34 with improving momentum and evidence that iPhone order reports are being revised upward. That is not a vague hope. That is a trigger.

So my challenge to the conservative view is this. You are overvaluing the need for a slower, safer exit and undervaluing the value of selling into the bounce while the stock still has a bid. You are trying to protect the firm, but you are still leaving too much tactical Apple risk in the book. My challenge to the neutral view is this. You are calling a reduced long position balanced, but it is still a directional bet that the demand story, the foldable launch, the multiple, and the macro tape all resolve in Apple’s favor. The trader’s sell proposal is the bolder and, in this case, the better plan. Sell AAPL exposure into strength between 338 and 344. If it holds 335 and starts showing a positive MACD histogram re cross with no further supply chain deterioration, we can reevaluate. If it closes below 335, exit the tactical position and do not try to negotiate with the tape. If we want to get back in, the clean trigger is a decisive reclaim above 345.34 with improving momentum and evidence that iPhone order reports are being revised upward. Until then, the aggressive edge is to take the risk out, keep the optionality, and let the market prove the bulls right before we pay for it again. That is the high reward path.
