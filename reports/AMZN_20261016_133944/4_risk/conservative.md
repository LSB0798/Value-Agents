The aggressive case is right that waiting for perfection can mean paying a worse price. I do not disagree with that. But the conservative job is not to catch the exact bottom. The conservative job is to protect the firm’s assets while preserving the ability to participate when the risk has actually decreased. Missing the first part of a move is an acceptable cost. Being trapped in a high beta name with a broken short term tape, an unresolved catalyst, negative free cash flow, rising debt, rising depreciation, regulatory friction, and a rotating sector is not. The better path is not to be first into every recovery. The better path is to be in the position when the market has removed some of the uncertainty, when the stop is more meaningful, and when the capital at risk is small enough that a gap does not become a portfolio problem. That is why I would not support the trader’s buy as written, and I would push for a much smaller, later, more conditional approach. My final transaction proposal is a conditional hold, not the original staged buy.
